# Design inspiration

Websites worth studying, in three groups. Open each one and **scroll slowly**. The motion is
what you're judging, and screenshots can't show it.

**Note:** These were found through design roundups and award listings. I haven't clicked through
each one yet, and sites change, so a few may look different now.

## 1. Award-winning hotel sites (the motion benchmark)

Awwwards is the main award site for web design. Its "Site of the Day" winners are the top tier.

- **Awwwards hotel and restaurant collection:** the best single place to browse.
  https://www.awwwards.com/websites/hotel-restaurant/
- **Awwwards luxury collection:** https://www.awwwards.com/websites/luxury/
- **Tengile (MalaMala Collection):** Site of the Day, Oct 2026. A safari lodge, which makes it a
  good model for slow, cinematic, nature-led scrolling.
- **White Desert:** Site of the Day, Sep 2026. Find it in the Awwwards hotel list.
- **The Drake Hotel:** Site of the Day, Sep 2025.

**What to borrow:** full-screen images that change as you scroll, text that fades or slides in
over photos, and slow pacing that makes the visitor feel calm.

## 2. Luxury hotel sites (the mood benchmark)

Your clients' own websites. Your portfolio should feel like it belongs alongside them.

- **Hotel 1, Place Vendôme (Paris):** room images in an endless scroll that also works by
  dragging sideways
- **Hotel Particulier (Paris):** text scrolls and animates over large photos
- **The Berkeley (London):** reviewers call out its smooth scroll animation
- **Gramercy Park Hotel (New York):** a scrolling slideshow of amenities and small details

**What to borrow:** large photography, lots of empty space, elegant serif headings, and
restrained color.

## 3. Hotel and interiors photographers (the business benchmark)

These are your competitors. Most are solid but **visually standard**, which is your opening.

| Photographer | Site | What they do well |
|---|---|---|
| Mike Kelley | https://www.mpkelley.com/ | Credits the architect on every project, which builds trust |
| Impressions Photography | https://www.impressionsphotography.org/hotel-photography-portfolio/ | Sorts work by shot type: interiors, aerials, lifestyle, spa, F&B |
| Seth Parker | https://sethparker.net/hotel-hospitality-photography/ | Shows the full guest journey and names real hotel clients |
| Dixie Dixon | https://www.dixiedixon.com/lifestyle-hotel | Lists recognizable brands (Marriott, Gaylord) |
| Vice Versa | https://viceversa.gr/hotel-photographer-portfolio/ | A separate page for each property |
| Arturo+Lauren | https://arturoandlauren.com/hotel-resort-photography | Combines twilight, drone, and lifestyle shots |

**What to borrow:** organize work by category, give each project its own page, and show
client names once you have them.

## Ideas to steal for your site

1. **Opening scene:** a full-screen hero photo that slowly zooms or shifts as you scroll, with
   your name fading in.
2. **Guest journey scroll:** one property told in order (arrival → lobby → room → dining →
   pool → aerial), with each image revealed as you scroll. It doubles as a sales pitch.
3. **Before/after slider:** a phone snapshot next to your final photo. This speaks directly to
   the "hotels lack good photos" problem you've seen in your job.
4. **Horizontal gallery:** a sideways-scrolling strip for drone shots.
5. **Category hover:** hover over "Interiors" and a preview image fades in behind the text.
6. **Marketer's angle:** show one photo cropped for Instagram, a website banner, and a
   booking site. No other photographer on this list does it.

**A caution from the research:** too much motion can annoy visitors and get in the way of the
photos. Hotel clients want to judge the spaces. Aim for **smooth and calm, not flashy**.
